How it works

Your plan in three steps

  1. 1

    Tell it your goal and where you train

    Lift, run a race, or both. A full gym, dumbbells at home, a hotel room with nothing in it. Two minutes of setup, then Forge knows.

  2. 2

    One tap builds your plan

    Your week, or the next 4 or 6, in one tap: every muscle on target, rest days in the right places, runs that build toward your race, and weights and paces that rise when you've earned them.

  3. 3

    Train anywhere. Change anything.

    Traveling? Tell Forge where you're staying and it rebuilds around what's there. Swap days, re-suggest a workout, re-plan the month. You're never locked in.

Forge Strength

A great workout from whatever's there

Each place keeps its own equipment, from nothing at all to a full gym. Pick the minutes and the muscles, or let Forge pick what's ready to train, and it builds the workout from what's actually in the room. Tell it which muscles come first and it does them early in the week.

  • Hotel room, hotel gym, garage, park, bands or a full gym
  • Priority focus: your top muscles get done first
  • Last time's weights carry over: "Last time 110 lb, keep 110 lb, aim for 12+"
  • 15 to 90 minutes, fitted to the time you have

Forge Run

A season built backward from your race

Give Forge a recent result and a goal race. It prices your paces, draws the season from general to specific, and plans each week to get there. It says whether the goal looks reachable, as a range and never as a promise.

  • A pace ladder of fifteen paces, from your results
  • The season funnel: general, support, specific, taper
  • Progression styles used by serious runners: tempo built up in stages, steady runs with a threshold finish, fartlek
  • 12 events, from the 60 m to the marathon, at three levels

Forge (Strength + Run)

One week, both kinds of training

Most apps plan your lifting or your running. Forge plans them in the same week, so a hard run doesn't land the day before heavy legs, and your strength work supports the race you're training for.

  • One schedule: lift days, run days and rest days, with the minutes you have
  • A day with a run and a lift plans the lift for the time that's left
  • Report an injury and Forge swaps the runs it rules out
  • Change your usual week and re-plan the weeks ahead in one tap

Why Forge exists

Why Forge exists

“It's motivating to see a 3D model of yourself looking good. It makes you want to do it.”

I train in different places. If I'm in Taiwan, I might only have a hotel room, but I still want my workout in, the right calories, and to see what I'll look like if I keep doing this workout and eating this food for four weeks or more.

I wanted to plan an ideal workout in any environment, weeks ahead, with the click of a button, and change it when I move. That's Forge. It knows your goals, anybody can use it, and it's fun, because you watch a 3D you get better and better.

The founder of Forge

Meet the founder

Science-first training, built by a 2:30 marathoner

I'm Bob. My first marathon took 3:58. I went deep into the physiology and the research, coached myself with periodized training, and got to 2:30: eight marathons under three hours, the fastest Texan at Boston in 2021, and a spot in the American Development Program at Chicago in 2022. I did it as a dad of three with a sales job that keeps me on the road, so every session had to count.

At 39 I'm putting the same science-first, periodized approach into strength, for health and the long run. Forge is how I train now: hotel gyms, early mornings, whatever time I've got. I'm still a marathoner at heart and I love coaching runners. Now we get to do this part together.

Privacy

Your body. Your data. Yours.

FAQ

Questions

When can I get Forge?

Forge is coming to the App Store for iPhone. You can get early access to the beta now through TestFlight, Apple's beta app: tap Join the beta, and on an iPhone Apple emails you the invite while seats last. Everyone who signs up is also on the launch list and gets an email when Forge launches.

Do I need a gym?

No. Tell Forge what's around you, from nothing at all to a full gym, and it builds from that: bodyweight moves in a hotel room, dumbbells at home, machines and cables at the gym. Each place keeps its own equipment.

How does Forge plan my workouts?

From your goal, your experience, the muscles you want to focus on, the days and minutes you have, and the equipment where you are. It counts the hard sets each muscle gets each week against a target, suggests more weight once you hit the top of your reps, and plans around what you actually did. With Forge Run it also plans your running from your race date and your paces.

Can I change the plan?

Any time. Swap two days, change where you're training and re-suggest, change a workout's minutes or swap an exercise. Change your usual week and re-plan the weeks ahead in one tap. Nothing is locked in.

Is Forge Run for beginners?

Yes. Tell Forge you're new and it starts with easy running and run-walk intervals, and builds a base before any hard sessions. If you race, it works backward from the race date. It never promises a time: it shows a likely range and what would change it.

Which events does Run cover?

Twelve: the 60 m, 100 m, 200 m, 400 m, 800 m, 1500 m, mile, 3K, 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon, at three levels, from a first finish to a performance season.

Does it work with my Garmin?

You can send a planned run or workout to a Garmin watch from the plan. It needs a Garmin watch and a Forge sign-in, and you turn it on in Settings. Forge doesn't record GPS runs: your watch or phone does that.

How does the 3D projection work?

Forge builds a 3D body from your measurements and projects it forward from your plan, training, protein and calories. It's an estimate based on published research: it shows a likely range, not a promise, and it updates as you log. Your results will differ.

Does it do nutrition, including low-carb and keto?

Yes, if you want it. Nutrition is optional. Turn it on for calorie and protein targets for your goal, and choose how much of your calories come from fat, up to 80% for low-carb and keto.

What does Forge cost?

Forge is free to download: the free version has your 3D character in a still pose, workouts and runs you log yourself, the exercise library, tape measurements and the weight log, and 3 one-tap workouts a week. In the US, Forge Strength is $9.99 a month or $59.99 a year, Forge Run is $19.99 a month or $119.99 a year, and Forge, both together, is $24.99 a month or $149.99 a year. Each yearly plan starts with a 2-week free trial for new subscribers. Forge Strength adds the projection, your character in motion, the 3D exercise videos, planning ahead, nutrition and sync; Forge Run adds a season plan built from your race, your pace ladder and sending runs to your Garmin watch.

What health data does Forge use?

Only what you give it: your measurements, your workouts and runs, and, if you turn it on, Apple Health. Your data stays on your phone and sync is end-to-end encrypted. Forge never sells it and has no ads.

Is Forge for kids?

Forge is for ages 11 and up. If you are under 13, it works only on your phone: no account, no sync and no marketing email.

Is my data private?

Your data stays on your phone and Forge works offline. Sync, part of Pro, is end-to-end encrypted: your data is locked on your phone before it's uploaded, so we can't read it. You can export a backup or delete your account any time.

How does setup work?

A short setup asks a few questions about your goal, your experience and where you train, and builds your first week from the answers. You can skip it and set everything up by hand.

Is Forge medical advice?

No. Forge is a training and wellness tool, not medical advice. If you have a health condition or an injury, or you're pregnant, check with your doctor before you start.
