How it works

Forge fits a curve of the speed a runner can hold for different lengths of time to your result. It fits a curve of the speed you can hold for different lengths of time to your result, so the answer is only as good as the result you give it: use a recent, all-out race or time trial on a flat road or track, not a hilly, treadmill or easy run.

Each training pace is then read from that curve. The race paces (mile, 3K, 5K, 10K, half marathon, marathon) are the speeds the curve gives at those distances. Threshold is the pace you could race for about 75 minutes (the half marathon pace, if your half takes less). Easy, steady and recovery paces are shares of your marathon speed: easy is 75 to 82 percent of it, steady 90 to 95 percent, recovery 68 to 75 percent.

The range column is the band around each pace, for the days you want some room: the ranges for the race paces are 2 percent either side.

Where the method comes from

  • Daniels J, Gilbert J (1979). Oxygen Power: Performance Tables for Distance Runners. The two published equations Forge uses for the oxygen cost of running at a speed and the share of that capacity a runner can hold for a given time.
  • Vickers AJ, Vertosick EA (2016). An empirical study of race times in recreational endurance runners. BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation 8:26. doi:10.1186/s13102-016-0052-y. Why the marathon is read cautiously without long runs.
  • Jones AM, Burnley M, Black MI, et al. (2019). The maximal metabolic steady state: redefining the "gold standard". Physiological Reports 7(10):e14098. doi:10.14814/phy2.14098. The critical-speed effort that stands in for the 10K pace of a slower 10K runner.

What it cannot tell you

These are estimates for training, not a medical or fitness test, and not a promise. A result from a different day, a hilly course or a poor race will move every number. Re-enter a newer result as you improve.

The curve is built from one result, so it cannot know whether you are better suited to short races or long ones. The more your result differs from the pace you are choosing, the less it says: trust the paces nearest your race distance most.

If your 10K takes 58 minutes or longer, the 10K row becomes a critical-speed effort between your 10K and 5K pace, and the page says so.
