How it works

Your estimated one-rep max is the weight times one plus the reps divided by 30. A set of 200 for 5 gives 200 × (1 + 5 ÷ 30) = 233.3.

The table runs the same sum the other way: the weight you could lift for a given number of reps is your one-rep max divided by one plus those reps divided by 30. The percent column is what that weight is of your one-rep max.

Forge counts any set of more than 12 reps as 12. A long set says little about a true max, and the formula drifts further off the longer the set.

Where the method comes from

  • Epley B (1985). Poundage chart. In Boyd Epley Workout. Lincoln, Nebraska: Body Enterprises. The formula, widely used in strength training.

What it cannot tell you

It is an estimate. It is most reliable for sets of about 10 reps or fewer, taken close to failure. A set far from failure, or a very long one, will read low or off.

Different lifts, and different people, fall away from the formula in different ways: it tends to be rougher for lifts with small muscles or a lot of technique. Use the table as a starting point and adjust to how the weight feels.

Do not test a true one-rep max alone, without a spotter or safeties, or when you are not warmed up. You do not need to test it to use the number.
