How it works

Pace is time divided by distance, and speed is distance divided by time. Choose whether to find your pace, your finish time or your distance, enter the other two, and press Calculate.

The splits show the clock at the end of every mile or kilometre if you hold the same pace the whole way. A mile is exactly 1,609.344 metres.

Where the method comes from

This one is arithmetic, not a model, so there is no estimate and no paper behind it.

What it cannot tell you

It assumes one steady pace from start to finish. Real races are not like that: hills, wind, heat, crowds and tiredness change the pace from mile to mile, and most people slow down late in a long race.

To see what a race result suggests you could run at other distances, use the race time predictor. For the paces to train at, use the training paces calculator.
