How it works

Forge fits a curve of the speed a runner can hold for different lengths of time to your result, and reads every other distance off it. It fits a curve of the speed you can hold for different lengths of time to your result, so the answer is only as good as the result you give it: use a recent, all-out race or time trial on a flat road or track, not a hilly, treadmill or easy run.

The likely range is Forge's own rule of thumb: about 1 percent either side of the time at the distance of your result, widening as the distance moves away from it (about 2 percent for a half marathon from a 5K, nearly 3 percent for a marathon from a short race). It is a design choice, not a measured chance of landing inside it.

A marathon predicted from a shorter race is read cautiously. Many recreational runners finish the marathon slower than their shorter races suggest, so unless you have a marathon on record or a recent long run, Forge adds a few percent to the marathon: 3 percent by default, 1.5 percent if your longest run of the last 8 weeks was 28 km (17.4 miles) or more.

Where the method comes from

  • Daniels J, Gilbert J (1979). Oxygen Power: Performance Tables for Distance Runners. The two published equations behind the curve.
  • Vickers AJ, Vertosick EA (2016). An empirical study of race times in recreational endurance runners. BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation 8:26. doi:10.1186/s13102-016-0052-y. The marathon caution.
  • Blythe DAJ, Király FJ (2016). Prediction and quantification of individual athletic performance of runners. PLoS ONE 11(6):e0157257. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0157257. Why runners' own results beat a fixed formula, and why two results beat one.

What it cannot tell you

A prediction is what your current fitness suggests if you are trained for that distance. It does not know your long runs, your fuelling, the course or the weather, and it is not a promise.

The further the distance from your result, the less it says. A 5K result is a good guide to a 10K and a rougher one to a marathon.
